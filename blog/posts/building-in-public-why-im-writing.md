Every studio I've worked at had the same invisible asset: the producer who knew *why* the last three launches slipped, which vendor actually delivers, and what the burndown chart wasn't telling you. That knowledge almost never gets written down. It leaves when they leave.

This blog is me writing mine down, in public.

## What you'll find here

I'm focusing on three threads that keep colliding in my day-to-day work:

- **AI-powered product management** — where LLMs genuinely compress producer workload (meeting synthesis, backlog hygiene, risk digests) and where they quietly make things worse.
- **Game production workflows** — the unglamorous machinery of shipping: build pipelines, milestone definitions, vendor management across time zones, live-ops cadences.
- **Building in public** — including this site. It's a hand-rolled HTML/CSS/JS single-page app with procedural canvas scenes, and this blog runs on it with no build step at all.

## Why in public?

Three reasons, honestly ranked:

1. Writing forces me to turn instinct into a checkable process. If I can't explain a workflow to a stranger, I don't actually understand it yet.
2. Producers work in the dark by default — our wins look like *nothing going wrong*. Public notes are how the discipline gets better collectively.
3. Accountability. A publish date is a milestone, and I respect milestones.

> "The palest ink is better than the best memory." — I've watched million-dollar decisions get re-litigated because nobody wrote the first one down.

## The cadence

One post every couple of weeks. Short, concrete, and honest about what didn't work. If you want these in your reader, there's an [RSS feed](/blog/feed.xml) — no algorithm, no newsletter pop-up.
